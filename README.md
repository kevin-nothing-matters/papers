# Papers

The Quantum Family Tree trilogy plus the Lemma D open problem.

## The trilogy

### Paper 1 — Emergence of Distance from a Quantum Family Tree

Shows that on a binary Bruhat–Tits tree with Haar-random U(4) gates on every bond, the quenched von Neumann entanglement entropy grows linearly with tree distance at rate c_VN = (9/10) log₂(5/2) ≈ 1.1897 bits per generation. Submitted to *Communications in Mathematical Physics*. Confirmed numerically at 10.34σ and on a 156-qubit IBM Heron run.

DOI: [10.5281/zenodo.19464058](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19464058)

### Paper 2 — The Emergence of Geometry from a Quantum Family Tree

Extends the entropy-rate theorem from binary qubits (p=2, d=2) to general p-ary trees with d-dimensional bonds. Proves four structural theorems: fixed-point convergence, first law at all scales, geometric precondition, and boundary-mode decomposition. The factor w₂ = (1−η₂)/2 is derived unconditionally from two-endpoint symmetry.

DOI: [10.5281/zenodo.19629206](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19629206)

### Paper 3 — Spacetime and the Quantum Family Tree

Treats the dynamic case: what happens when the tree grows. Establishes tree ancestry as an exact causal structure, tree growth as cosmological expansion with de Sitter scale factor, retarded Green's-function response to gate perturbations, and a monotone c-function under the holographic RG flow. Assembles the result into a Conjoined Theorem relating circuit dynamics to spacetime structure.

DOI: [10.5281/zenodo.19629257](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19629257)

## Open problem

### Lemma D — An invitation to the field

A standalone document stating the one remaining scalar identity — Lemma D — whose proof would close the trilogy. Includes a layman's explanation, the formal mathematical challenge, a catalog of dead routes, and an invitation to any researcher with tools that might apply.
